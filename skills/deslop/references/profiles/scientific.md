# Profile: scientific

Academic papers, abstracts, grant text, technical reports with citations.

**Goal:** precise and plain, not lively. The correct human voice here is neutral.

- **Do not inject personality, opinion, or first-person color.** Neutral *is* human here.
- **Keep legitimate hedging.** "These results suggest" reflects genuine scientific caution,
  not AI evasion. Strip *stacked* hedges (patterns §E2) and vague attributions (§A5) — but a
  single honest qualifier stays. Replace phantom authorities with real citations, or cut the claim.
- **Keep precise terminology and numbers.** Don't simplify a term to sound friendlier. Don't
  swap a number for an adjective.
- **Passive voice is often correct** (methods sections). Only fix §B7 when active voice is
  genuinely clearer and the field allows "we."
- **Kill hardest:** significance inflation (§A1), promotional adjectives (§A4), AI vocabulary
  (§B1: delve, intricate interplay, pivotal, underscore), rule-of-three (§B4), "it's worth
  noting" filler (§E1), symbolic gloss (§F3), the treadmill effect (§F1 — dense is good here).
- **Preserve:** citations, defined terms, equations, hedged claims that map to real uncertainty.
- No contractions. No engagement hooks. No generic upbeat conclusion — end on the finding.
